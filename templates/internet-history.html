Introduction

The Internet is a global system of interconnected computer networks that exchange data using shared protocols. It has no single inventor: it grew out of work by many researchers, among them Paul Baran, Donald Davies, Leonard Kleinrock, Vint Cerf, Bob Kahn and Jon Postel.

Its origins lie in ARPANET (1969), but the Internet in its modern sense dates from the adoption of TCP/IP in 1983. Each milestone below gives the year, the event and why it mattered; every date was verified against the source cited beneath it.

Milestone timeline

  1. 1969 — ARPANET's first connection

    On 29 October 1969 the first packet-switched message travelled from UCLA to Stanford Research Institute, and four nodes were linked by year's end. Why it mattered: it proved packet switching worked and that a network could survive the loss of individual nodes.

    Source: Internet Society — Brief History of the Internet: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

  2. 1971 — Network email

    Ray Tomlinson at BBN sent the first message between two computers and chose "@" to separate user from host. Why it mattered: email became the network's first mass-appeal application, and the user@host format is still in use.

    Source: MIT Lemelson Center — Ray Tomlinson: https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/ray-tomlinson

  3. 1983 — TCP/IP becomes the standard

    On 1 January 1983 ARPANET switched from NCP to TCP/IP, designed by Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn. Why it mattered: often called the Internet's birthday: separate networks could now interconnect into a network of networks.

    Source: Internet Society — Brief History of the Internet: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

  4. 1983 — Domain Name System

    Paul Mockapetris specified DNS in November 1983, replacing the central HOSTS.TXT file with a distributed naming system. Why it mattered: it made addresses human-readable and removed the bottleneck that had capped growth.

    Source: USC Information Sciences Institute — And the DNS was Born: https://www.isi.edu/news/972810/and-the-dns-was-born/

  5. 1985 — First registered domain name

    symbolics.com was registered on 15 March 1985 by Symbolics Inc., a Massachusetts computer manufacturer. Why it mattered: it marked the start of commercial presence online and of the domain-name industry.

    Source: Dynadot — The Complete History of Domain Names: https://www.dynadot.com/hub/domain-branding/history-of-domain-names

  6. 1989-1991 — The World Wide Web

    Tim Berners-Lee proposed a hypertext system at CERN in March 1989, built the first server and browser in 1990, and announced the software on 6 August 1991. Why it mattered: it added an easy information layer on top of the Internet, released into the public domain in 1993.

    Source: CERN — A short history of the Web: https://home.cern/science/computing/the-birth-of-the-web/short-history-web/

  7. 1993 — Mosaic browser

    Marc Andreessen and Eric Bina released Mosaic at NCSA, University of Illinois, on 23 January 1993. Why it mattered: its point-and-click interface brought non-technical users online and triggered the commercial boom.

    Source: National Science Foundation — Mosaic Launches an Internet Revolution: https://nsf.gov/news/mosaic-launches-internet-revolution

  8. 1994-1995 — Encryption and e-commerce

    Netscape introduced SSL in 1994, the first secure online purchase was made that August, and Amazon and eBay launched in 1995. Why it mattered: encryption made card payments viable and proved consumers would buy over a network.

    Source: HowStuffWorks — The History of E-commerce: https://money.howstuffworks.com/history-e-commerce.htm

  9. 1998 — Google

    Larry Page and Sergey Brin incorporated Google on 4 September 1998, using the PageRank algorithm developed at Stanford. Why it mattered: ranking pages by link structure made a fast-growing Web navigable and made search its main entry point.

    Source: Britannica Money — Google: https://www.britannica.com/money/Google-Inc

  10. 2004 — Facebook

    Launched from a Harvard dorm on 4 February 2004 and opened to the public in 2006. Why it mattered: it shifted the Internet from documents to social connection and established targeted advertising.

    Source: Britannica Money — Facebook: https://www.britannica.com/money/Facebook

  11. 2007 — iPhone and the mobile Internet

    Apple released the first iPhone on 29 June 2007, with a touchscreen and a full web browser. Why it mattered: access moved from desks to pockets; mobile devices now carry roughly 60% of global Internet traffic.

    Source: Maryville University — The Evolution of Social Media: https://online.maryville.edu/blog/evolution-social-media/
