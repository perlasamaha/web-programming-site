Introduction

The Internet is a global system of interconnected computer networks that exchange data using shared protocols. It has no single inventor: it grew out of work by many researchers, among them Paul Baran, Donald Davies, Leonard Kleinrock, Vint Cerf, Bob Kahn and Jon Postel.

Its origins lie in ARPANET (1969), but the Internet in its modern sense dates from the adoption of TCP/IP in 1983. Each milestone below gives the year, the event and why it mattered; every date was verified against the sources listed with it.

Milestone timeline

  1. ARPANET's first connection — 1969

    On 29 October 1969 the first packet-switched message travelled from UCLA to Stanford Research Institute, and four nodes were linked by year's end.

    Why it mattered: it proved packet switching worked and that a network could survive the loss of individual nodes.

    Source: Internet Society — Brief History of the Internet, internetsociety.org

  2. Network email — 1971

    Ray Tomlinson at BBN sent the first message between two computers and chose "@" to separate user from host.

    Why it mattered: email became the network's first mass-appeal application, and the user@host format is still in use.

    Source: MIT Lemelson Center — Ray Tomlinson, lemelson.mit.edu

  3. TCP/IP becomes the standard — 1983

    On 1 January 1983 ARPANET switched from NCP to TCP/IP, designed by Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn.

    Why it mattered: often called the Internet's birthday: separate networks could now interconnect into a network of networks.

    Source: Internet Society — Brief History of the Internet, internetsociety.org

  4. Domain Name System — 1983

    Paul Mockapetris specified DNS in November 1983, replacing the central HOSTS.TXT file with a distributed naming system.

    Why it mattered: it made addresses human-readable and removed the bottleneck that had capped growth.

    Source: USC Information Sciences Institute — And the DNS was Born, isi.edu

  5. First registered domain name — 1985

    symbolics.com was registered on 15 March 1985 by Symbolics Inc., a Massachusetts computer manufacturer.

    Why it mattered: it marked the start of commercial presence online and of the domain-name industry.

    Source: ICANN — Celebrating the Rise of the Modern Internet, icann.org

  6. SSL and encrypted connections — 1994

    Netscape introduced SSL in 1994 and the protocol was later documented by the IETF as RFC 6101.

    Why it mattered: encrypting a connection end to end made it safe to send card numbers and passwords across a public network.

    Source: IETF — RFC 6101, The SSL Protocol Version 3.0, rfc-editor.org

  7. iPhone and the mobile Internet — 2007

    Apple released the first iPhone on 29 June 2007, with a touchscreen and a full web browser.

    Why it mattered: access moved from desks to pockets; mobile devices now carry roughly 60% of global Internet traffic.

    Source: Maryville University — The Evolution of Social Media, online.maryville.edu

  8. Generative AI reaches the public — 2022

    OpenAI released ChatGPT on 30 November 2022; it passed a million users in five days and about 800 million weekly users by November 2025.

    Why it mattered: conversational AI became a second front door to the Internet alongside search, changing how people find information and how sites are written for machines as well as readers.

    Source: Britannica — ChatGPT, britannica.com

  9. Six billion people online — 2025

    The ITU reported on 17 November 2025 that about 6 billion people, roughly 74% of the world, use the Internet, while 2.2 billion remain offline and 5G covers 55% of the population.

    Why it mattered: growth is slowing and the remaining gap is now about affordability and skills rather than coverage, with 96% of the offline population in low- and middle-income countries.

    Source: ITU — Measuring digital development: Facts and Figures 2025, itu.int
